# 🛍️ Customer Shopping Behaviour Analysis

### End-to-End Data Analytics Project | Python • SQL • Power BI

## 📌 Project Overview

This project analyzes customer shopping behaviour using a retail dataset containing **3,900 purchase records and 18 columns**. 

The objective is to identify purchasing patterns, customer segments, product preferences, discount usage, and subscription behaviour to support data-driven business decisions.

The project follows an end-to-end analytics workflow: data cleaning and feature engineering in Python, business analysis using SQL, and interactive dashboard development in Power BI.

**Business question:** 

How can a retail company leverage consumer shopping data to identify trends, improve customer engagement, and optimize marketing and product strategies?

## 🎯 Business Objectives

- Analyze revenue contribution by gender and age group.
- Understand differences between subscribers and non-subscribers.
- Identify highly rated products and products with high discount usage.
- Compare purchase amounts across shipping methods.
- Segment customers based on previous purchase history.
- Examine repeat-purchase behaviour and subscription status.
- Develop an interactive dashboard to communicate customer behaviour insights.
- Translate analytical findings into actionable business recommendations.

## 🔄 Project Workflow

### 1. Data Cleaning & Feature Engineering — Python

Used Pandas and NumPy to explore and prepare the raw dataset for analysis.

**Key activities:**

- Inspected dataset structure, data types, and descriptive statistics.
- Identified missing values in the Review Rating column.
- Imputed missing ratings using the median rating within each product category.
- Standardized column names to snake_case.
- Created an `age_group` column by grouping customer ages.
- Created a `purchase_frequency_days` column from purchase-frequency categories.
- Compared `discount_applied` and `promo_code_used` to identify redundancy and removed the promotional-code column.
- Connected Python to the database and loaded the cleaned DataFrame for SQL analysis.

### 2. Database Integration & SQL Analysis

Loaded the prepared customer data into a structured database table and performed business analysis using SQL.

The analysis covered ten business questions:

1. **Revenue by Gender:** Compared total purchase revenue generated by male and female customers.
2. **High-Spending Discount Users:** Identified customers who used discounts but spent at least the average purchase amount.
3. **Product Ratings:** Ranked the five products with the highest average review ratings.
4. **Shipping Comparison:** Compared average purchase amounts for Standard and Express shipping.
5. **Subscription Analysis:** Compared customer counts, average spending, and total revenue between subscribers and non-subscribers.
6. **Discount Usage:** Identified the five products with the highest percentage of discounted purchases.
7. **Customer Segmentation:** Classified customers into New, Returning, and Loyal groups using previous-purchase counts.
8. **Top Products by Category:** Identified the three most-purchased products within each category.
9. **Repeat Buyers & Subscriptions:** Compared subscription status among customers with more than five previous purchases.
10. **Revenue by Age Group:** Calculated total purchase revenue contributed by each age group.

**SQL concepts applied:**

- Aggregate functions: `SUM()`, `AVG()`, `COUNT()`
- Filtering and grouping: `WHERE`, `GROUP BY`, `ORDER BY`
- Conditional logic: `CASE`
- Subqueries
- Common Table Expressions (CTEs)
- Window functions: `ROW_NUMBER()`

The following findings are based on the SQL outputs and Power BI dashboard documented in the project report.

### 1. Revenue by Gender

| Gender | Total Revenue |
|---|---:|
| Male | $157,890 |
| Female | $75,191 |

Male customers contributed more purchase revenue in this dataset. 
Further analysis of customer counts and average spending by gender would help distinguish differences in customer volume from differences in purchasing behaviour.

### 2. Subscriber vs. Non-Subscriber Behaviour

| Subscription Status | Customers | Average Spend | Total Revenue |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Yes | 1,053 | $59.49 | $62,645 |
| No | 2,847 | $59.87 | $170,436 |

Non-subscribers contributed more total revenue because they represented a larger share of the customer records. 
Average spending was slightly higher among non-subscribers in this dataset.

**Business implication:** Investigate subscription benefits, customer needs, and conversion opportunities before assuming that subscriptions increase spending or loyalty.

### 3. Top Five Products by Average Review Rating

| Product | Average Rating |
|---|---:|

| Sandals | 3.84 |

Gloves had the highest average review rating among the five products listed. These products may warrant further investigation for customer satisfaction and product positioning opportunities.

### 4. Average Purchase Amount by Shipping Type

| Shipping Type | Average Purchase Amount |

| Standard | $58.46 |

Express shipping customers had a higher average purchase amount than Standard shipping customers in the analyzed records. This is an association, not proof that express shipping causes higher spending.

### 5. Products with the Highest Discount Rates

Hats had the highest percentage of discounted purchases among the five listed products. Reviewing discount frequency alongside margins and sales volume could help assess promotional efficiency.

### 6. Customer Segmentation

| Loyal | 3,116 |
| Returning | 701 |

The SQL segmentation classified customers using previous-purchase counts: New customers had one previous purchase, Returning customers had between two and ten, and Loyal customers fell into the remaining category.

These are project-defined segments rather than a universal customer segmentation standard.

Among customers with more than five previous purchases:

The results show more non-subscribers than subscribers among the qualifying repeat-buyer records. To compare subscription likelihood fairly, the counts should also be expressed as proportions of all subscribers and all non-subscribers who meet the repeat-buyer criterion.

These results can guide further investigation into demographic preferences and campaign performance.

## 💡 Business Recommendations

Based on the documented findings, the following strategies are worth testing:

**1. Improve Subscription Conversion**

Investigate why non-subscribers account for a larger share of customers and revenue. Test relevant subscription benefits and measure conversion and retention outcomes.

**2. Strengthen Customer Loyalty**

Use the existing customer segments to design retention campaigns and rewards for repeat purchasers. Track repeat-purchase rates over time to evaluate effectiveness.

**3. Review Discount Strategy**

Examine the products with the highest discount rates alongside sales volume, profit margins, and customer response before changing promotional budgets.

**4. Improve Product Positioning**

Use product ratings and purchase counts to identify products for merchandising, product-page improvements, and targeted campaigns.

**5. Evaluate Shipping Preferences**

Investigate whether the higher average purchase amount among Express shipping customers persists after accounting for customer demographics and product categories.

**6. Refine Marketing by Age Group**

Use age-group revenue as a starting point for campaign analysis. 
Evaluate conversion rates, customer acquisition costs, and profitability before allocating additional marketing spend.
